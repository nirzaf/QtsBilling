# QB-31 — Shared commercial amount contract and the inclusive-tax posting fix

**Status: DONE (implemented, unit-tested and verified end to end on PostgreSQL).**

This is the first slice of the *SMB Completion and Production Hardening* specification (task 03 and the
critical part of task 12). It fixes the two highest-severity commercial defects the specification names:

| Finding | Severity | Before | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| F11 — inclusive POS totals credited the tax twice in the journal | Critical | a tax-inclusive line of 115 at 15% posted `DR 115 / CR revenue 115 + CR tax 15` — the entry did not balance | `DR 115 / CR revenue 100 + CR tax 15` — the entry balances |
| F12 — the device/UI tax calculation always added the tax and had no canonical rounding | High | `quantity × unitPrice × taxRate`, unrounded, exclusive only | the server, the browser and the device call one shared calculation with the tax mode and one rounding rule |

## What was built

| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `QtsModels/ValueObjects/CommercialAmounts.cs` | The one commercial calculation: pure, deterministic, checked. Per line it computes the gross `G = Round4(q × p)`, the authorized discount `D ≤ G`, the revenue base `N`, the tax `T` and the payable `L`. **Exclusive**: `N = G − D`, `T = Round4(N × r)`, `L = N + T`. **Inclusive**: `L = G − D`, `T = Round4(L × r / (1 + r))`, `N = L − T`. **Exempt**: no tax. The identity `N + T = L` therefore holds in every mode. The document totals are the sums of the already-rounded lines, a header discount is allocated in whole money units with a deterministic remainder, and the settlement rounding is an explicit amount. |
| `QtsBilling.Contracts/Pos/CommercialAmountCalculator.cs` | A thin typed facade over the calculation for the server, the browser and the device. It contains no arithmetic of its own and never coerces an unknown tax mode into a default. |
| `QtsModels/ValueObjects/CommercialAmounts.cs` (validation) | Amounts that do not fit the declared scale or range are **rejected**, never rounded: the quantity at six decimals, the price and the discount at four, the rate in `[0, 1)`, the values inside `numeric(18,4)`/`numeric(18,6)`. All derived rounding is `MidpointRounding.AwayFromZero`; the framework's banker's rounding is never mixed in. |
| `QtsBilling.Persistence.Postgres/Entities/Sales.cs` + the migration `20261019214732_AddSaleLineTaxMode` | The tax composition of a posted line is stored as a snapshot. The migration backfills the existing lines from the product they refer to (an exact read-back, not an invented value) and never rewrites a historical document. |
| `QtsBilling.Persistence.Sqlite/Entities/CacheEntities.cs` + the migration `20261019215209_AddCachedProductTaxMode` | The device cache carries the tax mode of the product, so the offline calculation uses the same rule as the server. |
| `QtsWebApp/QtsWebApp/Features/Sales/SalePosting.cs` | The posting path now uses the shared calculation, and the journal credits the **revenue base** (`total − tax`) instead of the gross. That is the actual fix of F11. |
| `QtsBilling.Ui/Pages/Pos.razor.cs` | The device checkout computes the line with the shared calculation and the cached tax mode. |
| `QtsWebApp/QtsWebApp/Features/Sales/ReceiptRenderer.cs` | A tax-inclusive line says so on the receipt: "tax 15.0000 (15%) included in the price". |

## Evidence

**Unit level** — `TestQtsBilling/ValueObjects/CommercialAmountsTests.cs` (17 cases): the inclusive
115 at 15% → revenue 100 / tax 15 / payable 115; the exclusive 100 at 15% → 100 / 15 / 115; a mixed
document balances across the modes; a fractional quantity is exact; a half-way value rounds away from
zero (`0.5 × 0.2469 = 0.12345 → 0.1235`, never `0.1234`); a full discount is allowed and leaves no
revenue; an excessive precision, a negative value, an undefined tax mode and a value beyond the ledger
range are rejected; the header discount is allocated in whole money units and is reproducible; the
device and the server calculate the same amount.

**System level** — a tax-inclusive line of 115 at 15% posted against the development PostgreSQL
(the `Idempotency-Key` header, the real API, the real journal):

```
 entry_number  | line_no | code | debit_amount | credit_amount
---------------+---------+------+--------------+----------------
 JE-2026-00006 |       1 | 1000 |     115.0000 |        0.0000   (cash)
 JE-2026-00006 |       2 | 4000 |       0.0000 |      100.0000   (revenue)
 JE-2026-00006 |       3 | 2100 |       0.0000 |       15.0000   (tax payable)
 JE-2026-00006 |       4 | 5000 |      60.0000 |        0.0000   (cost of goods sold)
 JE-2026-00006 |       5 | 1200 |       0.0000 |       60.0000   (inventory)

entry balance (debits - credits): 0.0000
stored line: quantity 1.0000, unit_price 115.0000, tax_rate 0.1500, tax_amount 15.0000,
             line_total 115.0000, tax_mode Inclusive
```

Before the fix the same sale produced `CR revenue 115` next to `CR tax 15`, so the entry was out of
balance by the tax amount.

**Regression** — the whole solution builds; 95 tests pass (58 in `TestQtsBilling`, 30 in the SQLite
store suite, 7 in the import suite); the secret scan is clean.

## Decisions recorded

- **D-58** — one commercial calculation (task 03): the server, the browser and the Windows device use
  the same pure function; the identity `revenue base + tax = payable` holds in every tax mode; a
  value that does not fit the declared scale is rejected instead of rounded; the tax composition of a
  posted line is an immutable snapshot.
- **D-59** — the journal composition (task 12): a posting credits the **revenue base**, never the
  gross. A tax-inclusive line is not a special case in the ledger: it only differs in how the tax
  inside the payable is derived.

## Still open in this slice

- The **discount policy** (who may approve a discount of which size) is not implemented: the calculation
  accepts an authorized discount amount, but the approval limit is still to be defined with the
  permission model (task 12, remaining part).
- The **settlement scale** is per business in the calculation (0…4 decimals) but the business settings
  do not carry it yet (task 08).
- The **stock valuation** is still the catalog purchase price, not a perpetual average (task 15).
