# QB-35 — A goods receipt is recognised in the ledger (F16)

**Status: DONE (the receipt posts a balanced entry; verified against the real PostgreSQL).**

This is the critical part of task 16 of the *SMB Completion and Production Hardening* specification. It
closes the critical finding **F16**: a goods receipt moved the **quantity** but posted **no journal
entry**, so the stock account and the general ledger disagreed about the value of the stock.

## What changed

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| The receipt created the stock movement and the balance row. Nothing else. The inventory account of the business never moved, and the value of the received goods was nowhere in the ledger. | The receipt posts a balanced entry in the same transaction as the movement: **DR inventory (1200) / CR goods received not invoiced (2101)** for a receipt, and **DR goods received not invoiced / CR inventory** for a return. |

The recognition follows the matrix of the specification (D08):

- a **purchase order posts nothing** — it is only a commitment;
- a **goods receipt** capitalises the stock and clears it — the value waits in the clearing account
  until the supplier bill arrives, so it is neither a supplier payable nor invisible;
- a **return** relieves the stock and clears it again, at the same cost the receipt carried.

The chart of accounts is verified before the posting: a business without an active inventory account
(`1200`) and a clearing account (`2101`) gets a clear `409` instead of a receipt without a ledger
effect.

## Evidence

`TestQtsBilling/Integration/PurchasingReceiptPostingTests.cs` — the real host, the real PostgreSQL:

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| T16.01 — a goods receipt of 10 pieces at 60 | the inventory account carries **+600**, the clearing account **+600**, the **payables account is untouched** (0) — a receipt is not a supplier payable — and every journal entry balances. |
| T16.02 — the quantity and the value tie | the stock balance grew by exactly the received quantity and equals the sum of the immutable movements; every entry balances. |

## Decisions recorded

- **D-64** — the receipt recognition (task 16): a goods receipt posts a balanced entry in the same
  transaction as the stock movement — the value is capitalised in the inventory and cleared in the
  goods-received-not-invoiced account. A purchase order still posts nothing, and a return reverses
  the same clearing. The chart of accounts is verified before the posting, so a business can never
  receive stock that is invisible to its own ledger.

## Still open in this slice

- The **supplier bill** that clears the account does not exist yet (task 45): the clearing balance is
  carried by the account, not by an open item.
- The **purchase-price variance** of a return (the difference between the current stock value and the
  original supplier consideration) is not an explicit amount yet; it becomes one with the supplier
  bill.
- The **input tax** on the receipt is not recognised (the bill carries it, task 45).
